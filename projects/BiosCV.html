WHY BOA VISTA

Where conservation begins on the sand

Boa Vista is one of the world's most important nesting areas for the loggerhead sea turtle (Caretta caretta). Protecting its beaches requires sustained field monitoring and a coordinated effort by conservation staff and volunteers.

THE ORGANISATION

BIOS Cabo Verde is a conservation organisation working on marine and coastal biodiversity through research, monitoring, education and community engagement. Its sea turtle programme includes nesting beach patrols, tagging, nest monitoring and hatchling protection.

FIELD NOTES · LIFE AT CAMP

A camp beneath an old acacia.

For four weeks, our home was a field camp on the southern coast of Boa Vista. An enormous old acacia stood at its centre: between its trunks and branches, we built and sheltered tents, cooking areas and shared spaces. Life was simple, communal and organised around the rhythm of the beach.

Beyond the camp lay approximately six kilometres of largely untouched coastline that our team patrolled and helped protect from poaching. Once night fell, the beach became our workplace.

WHAT I DID

From tracks to data.

These are the tasks I personally carried out as a conservation volunteer in 2018 — distinct from the broader research programme run by BIOS Cabo Verde.

01 / BEACH PATROLS

Following the nesting tracks

Patrolled beaches at night to locate nesting females and nesting activity, helped protect the monitored coastline, and carried out turtle censuses by counting tracks left in the sand.

02 / ADULT TURTLES

Marking and measuring

Assisted with the marking and morphometric measurement of nesting sea turtles and recorded field observations, including counts of eggs in clutches.

03 / NESTS & HATCHLINGS

Protecting the next generation

Located, monitored and protected nests; helped relocate clutches to a protected hatchery when needed; and collected and measured hatchlings as part of the monitoring work.

04 / DATA & OUTREACH

Beyond the night patrol

Collected scientific field data, organised and cleaned monitoring databases, and contributed to environmental education activities connected with sea turtle conservation.

A PERSONAL FIELD EXPERIENCE

Behind each night on the beach

Conservation fieldwork is built on many small, repeated tasks: walking the same stretch of sand, reading tracks, checking nests, recording measurements carefully and returning to camp to make sure the data are usable.

Working alongside the BIOS team brought together direct contact with wildlife, collective responsibility for a vulnerable nesting coastline, and the less visible but essential work of organising ecological information.
